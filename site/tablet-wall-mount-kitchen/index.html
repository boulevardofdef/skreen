How to mount a tablet on the kitchen wall.

A tablet on the wall only works if it stays charged, stays put and sits where you glance at it without leaning over the sink. Here are the choices, and the few details that matter.

Pick the spot first

  • Eye level for the tallest person who uses it, near where the family passes: by the fridge, the back door or the breakfast counter.
  • Away from the stove and the sink. Steam, grease and splashes are the main hazards. A spot a step or two from the action works better than one right above it.
  • Out of direct sun, which washes out the screen and heats the tablet.
  • Close to a power outlet, or somewhere you can run a cable neatly. A wall display lives on its charger.

Four ways to mount it

  1. A stand on the counter. The simplest. No holes, easy to move, and you can try the spot for a week first. The cable stays plugged in at the back.
  2. A wall mount or tablet frame. A bracket or frame screwed to the wall, with a gap or cut-out for the cable. Sold for most common tablet sizes; check the listing for your exact model, since cases and thick bezels matter.
  3. An adhesive or magnetic mount. No tools, good for renters. Check the weight rating against your tablet, and test it on your wall surface: textured paint and tile can defeat adhesives.
  4. A built-in or flush mount. A recess in the wall with power behind it. The cleanest result, and a job for a handy friend or an electrician.

Tablets don’t follow one standard. Unlike TVs, they have no common mounting holes, so buy a mount made for your tablet’s size, or an adjustable one with a good clamp. Measure first.

Power and cables

  • Use a long, good-quality cable and a charger rated for the tablet. Cheap chargers can be slow enough that a busy screen drains faster than it charges.
  • Hide the cable with a cord cover painted to match the wall, or route it behind the mount.
  • Keep it plugged in for an always-on display. Many tablets manage their charging to protect the battery. A very old tablet with a swollen battery shouldn’t be left charging. If the case bulges or the screen lifts, stop using it.

Landscape or portrait?

Skreen works in either direction and is designed for landscape on wall displays, so a wide mount usually looks best. In landscape, the clock, schedule and to-do list sit side by side.

Set the screen up for wall life

  • In the tablet’s settings, set the screen to stay on while charging, and lower the brightness. A wall display doesn’t need to be bright.
  • Skreen’s night mode dims the screen after dark, and the layout shifts slightly through the day, so the screen isn’t showing one frozen image for months.
  • Optional: make Skreen the Home screen, so it returns by itself after a restart.

Questions

What is the cheapest way to mount a tablet on a wall?

A stand on the counter costs the least and needs no tools. An adhesive or magnetic mount is the cheapest way to get it onto the wall without drilling, if your wall surface suits it.

Can I leave a tablet plugged in all the time?

Most modern tablets handle it fine. Don’t leave a tablet with a swollen battery or a lifting screen charging; stop using it.

Does my tablet need to be new?

No. Any Android tablet with Google Play, running Android 8 or newer, can run Skreen. Amazon Fire tablets don’t work, and an iPad version is planned but not yet available.
