A dedicated family calendar, or an app on a tablet you already own?

Digital family calendars come in two kinds: a purpose-built screen you buy, and an app that turns a tablet or screen into one. Neither is always better. Here is how to choose.

Prices, features and subscription terms change often. We describe categories rather than quote other companies’ prices, so check each product’s own page before you decide. Skreen is our app, so we are not neutral, and we’ve tried to be fair.

At a glance

  • Cost. A dedicated device means buying a screen, and some sell extra features as a monthly or yearly subscription. An app on a tablet you already own costs nothing extra for the hardware. Skreen is free, with one optional $14.99 upgrade and no subscription.
  • Setup. Dedicated devices usually arrive ready to mount, with their own app. A tablet app means you supply the tablet, a charger and a mount.
  • Screen quality and size. You choose the screen with an app: any tablet size, and your own mount. Dedicated devices come in the sizes the maker sells.
  • Where the data lives. Dedicated devices generally work through the maker’s account and servers. Skreen has no account or server for your calendar: events come from your Google account straight to the tablet.
  • Support and warranty. A dedicated device comes with a hardware warranty. With an app, the tablet’s warranty is whatever it already has.
  • Platforms. Skreen runs on Android tablets today, with an iPad version planned. It doesn’t run on Amazon Fire tablets. Check what each alternative supports.

Who should buy a dedicated device

If you don’t have a spare tablet, don’t want to pick a mount, and prefer a single product with its own support line, a purpose-built device is the lower-effort route.

Who should use an app on a tablet

If there is an old tablet in a drawer, if you’d rather not pay a subscription, or if you want your calendar to stay in your Google account, an app is the cheaper and more private path. See our guides to repurposing an old tablet and mounting it on the wall.

Questions to ask about any option

  1. What does it cost over three years, including any subscription?
  2. Which calendars does it support: Google, Apple, Outlook, school feeds?
  3. Can everyone in the family have their own view?
  4. Who holds the data, and can you delete it?
  5. What happens to it if the company changes its plans?

Questions

Is an app on an old tablet really cheaper than a dedicated calendar?

If you already own the tablet, the hardware is free and Skreen’s main display costs nothing. Add a stand or mount if you want one. Compare the three-year cost of any dedicated device, including any subscription, against that.

What does Skreen not do?

It is not a voice assistant, doesn’t control smart-home devices and doesn’t make video calls. It runs on Android tablets with Google Play; an iPad version is planned.

Where does my calendar data go?

From your Google account straight to the tablet. Skreen has no account system, and calendar content isn’t stored on a Skreen server.
